Track a status page until it changes.

Visa applications, loan approvals, orders and repairs often sit on 'Processing' for days. You keep logging in to look. Let one tab do the looking and tell you when the status moves.

Al Kafi SohagNov 19, 20262 min read
Add to Chrome Add to Firefox

Choose what to watch

Open the status page after you sign in. Note the exact word it shows now, such as "Processing", "In review" or "Received". When the status changes, that word will disappear. That is what you watch for.

Set up the alert

  1. Open the status page and the Free Auto Refresh popup.
  2. Under Advanced settings, Watch the page, choose Stop when text disappears and type the current status word.
  3. Choose an interval of 15 to 30 minutes. Statuses do not change by the second.
  4. Press Start.

A notification and a flashing tab title tell you when it changes.

Skip the manual reloads

Free Auto Refresh reloads a tab on a timer and can alert you when a page changes. Free, private, no account.

Mind the sign-in

Many status pages log you out after a few minutes of inactivity, and a reload then lands on the sign-in page. If that happens, the watch will report a change. Check the tab when you are alerted, and sign in again. Turn on a time limit so the tab does not run for days.

Keep your details private

Free Auto Refresh stores its settings on your device and makes no network requests of its own. It does not read or send the contents of your status page anywhere. See our privacy page.

What it does not do

It does not contact the office, speed up a decision or log in for you. It only saves you the repeated checking.

Good to know

Questions, answered.

How often should I check a visa or application status?

Every 15 to 30 minutes is plenty. Decisions are not made by the minute.

What if the site logs me out?

The reload will show the sign-in page and the watch will alert you. Sign in again and restart it.

Does the extension see my application details?

It runs on the page in your browser and stores nothing outside your device. It makes no network requests.
