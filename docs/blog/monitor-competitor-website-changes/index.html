Monitoring

Track competitor page changes.

A new price, a rewritten headline or a new plan can tell you a lot. You do not need a big tool to watch a few important pages.

Al Kafi SohagNov 27, 20262 min read

Pick a short list

  • Pricing and plan pages.
  • The main heading and intro on key landing pages.
  • Changelog, news or release pages.
  • An article that ranks where you want to rank.

Five useful pages you actually review beat a hundred you ignore.

Set up a watch in your browser

  1. Open the page, then open Free Auto Refresh and go to Advanced settings.
  2. Under Watch the page, choose to stop when the content changes.
  3. Add a CSS selector, such as h1 or the pricing section's class, to ignore the rest.
  4. Choose a long interval, for example 30 minutes or more, and press Start.

When it matches, refreshing stops and you get a desktop notification.

What a browser watcher cannot see

Page watching reads the visible content in your own browser. Do not expect it to report changes to title tags, meta descriptions, structured data or redirects. It also runs only while the tab is open and the computer is awake, and it keeps no long history of versions. If you need round-the-clock checks, many URLs, SEO tags or team reports, use a dedicated monitoring service.

Keep a simple change log

When you get an alert, note the date, the page, what changed and a screenshot. After a few weeks you can compare their changes with shifts you see in search results. A change happening first does not prove it caused a ranking move.

Be a polite visitor

Follow each site's terms, use slow intervals and watch public pages only. See rate limits and blocks.

Good to know

Questions, answered.

Can this watch meta titles?

Not dependably. Page watching is meant for what you can see on the page. Use an SEO crawler or monitoring service for tags.

How often should I check?

For most competitor pages, a few times a day or less is plenty.

Why do I get alerts when nothing important changed?

Rotating banners, dates and counters change the page. Narrow the watch with a CSS selector.
