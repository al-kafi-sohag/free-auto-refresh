Monitoring

Get notified when a web page changes.

Whether it is a price, a policy, a result or a stock level, you can have the page tell you when it changes. These are the free options and where each one falls short.

Al Kafi SohagNov 18, 20262 min read
Add to Chrome Add to Firefox

1. The site's own alert

Check first whether the site has an email or app alert. It is the most reliable option, because the site knows when the change happens. Look for "notify me" or "follow" buttons.

2. An RSS or news feed

Blogs, news and some stores publish feeds. A feed reader tells you about new items without you visiting. Many pages have none, though.

3. A hosted monitoring service

Online services check pages for you, even with your computer off. Free plans are usually limited to a few pages or slow check intervals.

4. A browser extension that watches the page

Free Auto Refresh reloads the page and stops with a notification when your text appears, when it disappears, or when the content changes. You can limit the watch to one element with a CSS selector such as #price, so ads and clocks do not cause false alarms.

The trade-off: the browser must stay open with the tab loaded. Everything is checked locally and nothing is sent anywhere.

5. Checking by hand

Still valid for pages you rarely care about. For anything urgent, automate it.

Which should you pick?

Use the site's own alert if there is one. For pages without it, use a hosted service for set-and-forget checks, or an extension when you want instant, private alerts and the page is already open.

Good to know

Questions, answered.

Can I monitor a page for a word?

Yes. Free Auto Refresh can stop and notify when text appears or disappears.

Does it work when my computer is off?

No. An extension needs the browser open. Hosted services work around that.

Does it read my pages?

It reads the visible text in your own browser and sends nothing out.
