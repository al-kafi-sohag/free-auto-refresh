Ticket alerts for sale openings and returns.

There are two moments worth catching: the sale opening, and returned tickets after a sell-out. Each needs a different approach, and one thing you should never do.

Al Kafi SohagNov 18, 20262 min read
Add to Chrome Add to Firefox

Moment 1: waiting for a sale to start

Watch the event page for the text that changes when the sale opens, such as "On sale now", "Buy tickets" or the disappearance of "Coming soon". Use Stop when text appears or disappears, with a 30 to 60 second interval and notifications on.

Moment 2: returned tickets

After a sell-out, tickets are sometimes released when holds expire. Watch for "Sold out" to disappear or for a purchase button to appear, at a gentle 1 to 2 minute interval.

Never refresh inside a waiting room

Many ticketing queues warn that reloading the page can lose your place. Once you are in a waiting room, stop the timer. Use refreshing only on the event page before the queue, not inside it.

Prepare before the alert

  • Sign in and save your payment details in advance.
  • Keep the tab open, and keep the computer awake.
  • Press Alt + Shift + R to stop the timer quickly.

What it does not do

It does not join queues, solve checks or buy for you. Follow the seller's terms. Our ticket sales guide has the exact settings.

Good to know

Questions, answered.

Should I refresh a ticket queue?

No. Many queues warn that it can lose your place. Stop the timer once you are inside.

Does it buy tickets for me?

No. It alerts you. You buy.

How often should it check?

Thirty seconds to two minutes, depending on how soon the sale opens.
