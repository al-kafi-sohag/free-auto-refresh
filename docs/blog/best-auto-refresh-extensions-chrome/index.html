Choosing an auto refresh extension for Chrome.

Several refresh extensions exist, and they differ in features, price and privacy. Instead of a ranked list, here is a checklist you can apply to any of them.

Al Kafi SohagNov 18, 20262 min read
Add to Chrome Add to Firefox

Disclosure

We make Free Auto Refresh, so we cannot be neutral. This page gives you the questions to ask. Check every extension, ours included, against them.

1. Interval range and randomness

Can you go down to a second, and up to hours? Is a random range included, or sold as a paid extra? A random interval looks less robotic and is gentler on sites.

2. One timer per tab

If one timer controls everything, a fast refresh on one page affects the rest. Per-tab timers avoid that.

3. Page watching and alerts

Reloading is half the job. The useful half is knowing when something changed. Look for text and change detection, an element picker and notifications.

4. Limits and pause

Can it stop after a time or number of refreshes? Can it pause while you type so a form is not wiped? These save you from the classic "forgot it was running" problem.

5. Price and account

Is it free, or free with paid extras? Does it need an account? Free Auto Refresh is free with no account or paid tier.

6. Permissions and privacy

Read the permissions list before installing. An extension that reads every page and sends data away needs a good reason. Free Auto Refresh has no analytics and processes watching locally in your browser.

Questions, answered.

Which auto refresh extension is best?

It depends on what you need. Use the checklist: intervals, per-tab timers, watching and alerts, limits, price and privacy.

Is a free auto refresh extension good enough?

For most people, yes. Free Auto Refresh includes random intervals, page monitoring, limits and smart pause at no cost.

Are refresh extensions safe?

Check permissions and whether the extension collects data. Prefer one that is open about both.
