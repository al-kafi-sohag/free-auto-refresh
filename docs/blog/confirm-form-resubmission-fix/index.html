Confirm form resubmission: stop before you click.

You pressed refresh or Back and the browser asks you to confirm form resubmission. It is not just a nuisance. Clicking the wrong button can send your form a second time.

Al Kafi SohagNov 27, 20262 min read
Add to Chrome Add to Firefox

What the message means

The page on your screen was the result of sending a form. To show it again, the browser would have to send the same data again. It will not do that without asking. When you use the Back button, Chrome may show ERR_CACHE_MISS with the same warning.

Should you press Continue?

Not if the form did something you do not want done twice. That includes payments, orders, bookings, messages and applications. Press Cancel, then check your order history, account page or email to see whether the first attempt worked.

For something harmless, such as a search or a filter, sending it again is usually fine.

How to get a clean copy of the page

  1. Click in the address bar.
  2. Press Enter. This opens the address as a normal visit instead of resending the form.
  3. If the page needs the form data to show anything, go to the site's menu and open the section again.

Do not auto refresh a form result page

A timer reloads whatever is in the tab. On a page that came from a form, that means running into this warning again. Start auto refresh only on a page you reached through a normal link or address, such as an order status page opened from your account. See how to track a status page.

Fixing it on your own site

Developers can prevent the warning with the Post/Redirect/Get pattern: handle the form with a POST, then answer with a redirect to a normal page. Refreshing then reloads that normal page and sends nothing again. A 303 status code is the usual choice for the redirect. Also protect payments on the server so that a repeated request cannot charge twice.

Good to know

Questions, answered.

Is ERR_CACHE_MISS a virus or a broken browser?

Usually not. It most often means the browser cannot show the old page without sending the form data again.

Can I turn the warning off?

It is a safety feature, so browsers do not offer a simple setting to disable it. Opening the address again is the safe way past it.

I clicked Continue on a payment page. What now?

Check your bank or card statement and the seller's order history. If you see two charges, contact the seller.
