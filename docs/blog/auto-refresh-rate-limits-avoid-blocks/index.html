How-to

Refresh politely, avoid the blocks.

If a site shows 'Too many requests' or slows you down, it is protecting itself from fast, repeated loads. These habits keep your refreshing well inside what most sites tolerate.

Al Kafi SohagNov 18, 20262 min read

What a rate limit is

A site counts how many requests come from you in a short time. Go over its limit and it may show an error (often 429 Too many requests), slow down, or temporarily block you. Limits differ per site.

1. Use a longer interval

The single biggest fix. Moving from 5 seconds to 30 usually removes the problem and rarely makes you miss a change. See our guide to the best refresh interval.

2. Use a random range

A min and max such as 30 to 60 seconds spreads requests out, instead of hitting the site on an even beat.

3. Set a limit

With refresh limits, stop after a time or a count. A refresh that ends after 30 minutes is far less likely to trouble anyone than one left running overnight.

4. Pause while you work

Smart pause holds the timer while you type, so you are not reloading and typing at once.

5. Respect the site's terms

Some sites forbid automated refreshing altogether. If a site's rules say no, do not refresh it. This is advice about being considerate, not a way around protections.

Good to know

Questions, answered.

What does 'Too many requests' mean?

You reloaded faster than the site allows. Wait a few minutes and use a longer interval.

Will a random interval stop it?

It is gentler on the site, but the best fix is a longer interval and a stop limit.

How long should I keep a refresh running?

As short as the job needs. Use a time limit so it ends by itself.
