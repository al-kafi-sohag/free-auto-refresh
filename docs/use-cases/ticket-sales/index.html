How ticket drops works

Practical guide

A ticket alert for returns and resale

Sold-out events often release a few more tickets: returns, production holds, face-value resale. They appear without warning and go quickly. Leaving the event page on a gentle auto refresh with a watch rule means you hear about it without sitting on the page.

Set it up to be safe

  • Never refresh inside a queue or waiting room. Reloading there can lose your place.
  • Use a random range, such as 20 to 45 seconds, and a time limit.
  • Watch the sold-out wording with Stop when text disappears.
  • Leave Pause while typing on so a reload never interrupts checkout.

Check the ticket site's rules

Many ticket sellers limit automated activity and may slow or block browsers that reload too often. Read their terms and keep to moderate intervals.

Use intervals that are reasonable for the site and only monitor pages you are allowed to access. The extension does not get around logins, queues, rate limits or other access restrictions.

A worked example: returned tickets

The event page says No tickets currently available. Sign in first, so you are ready to buy. Set Min 20 and Max 45, choose Stop when text disappears with that sentence, and set Stop after to 120 minutes. Press Start. If tickets are released, you get a notification and the tab title flashes; you then choose seats and pay yourself.

What it will not do

  • It will not hold your place in a queue.
  • It will not select seats or check out.
  • It will not get past purchase limits or bot checks.

If nothing happens

Check the watched sentence still matches the page exactly, and that the site has not signed you out or shown a verification page. A verification page counts as the text disappearing, so you will be alerted to that too.

At a glance

  • Hands off the F5 key. The page reloads on its own while you get on with something else.
  • Alert on availability. Watch for 'Sold out' to disappear or 'Find tickets' to appear.
  • Polite timing. Use a random range and a time limit, so you are not hammering the site all night.
Good to know

Questions, answered.

Will it buy tickets for me?

No. It reloads the page and alerts you. You buy them yourself.

Can it skip the queue?

No, and you should not run it while queuing.

How fast can I refresh a ticket page?

Technically every second, but 20 to 45 seconds is far less likely to get you blocked.

Does it work with resale pages?

Yes, on any page where availability is shown as text.

Will it buy the ticket for me?

No. It only reloads and alerts. You complete the purchase yourself.
